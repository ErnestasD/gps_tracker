# Teltonika completeness audit

Scope: `packages/codec`, `apps/ingest`, the AVL dictionaries and every reader that turns a stored `attrs` key back into a number (`apps/worker/src/normalize.ts`, `rules/*`, `trip/*`, `packages/db/src/{can,fuel,health,positions}.ts`, `apps/api/src/routes/crud.ts`, `apps/web/src/lib/telemetry.ts`, `packages/shared/params`). Paths are repo-relative to `/Users/ernis/Sites/gps_tracker`; the audit worktree was `9c98cf9` and `git diff 9c98cf9 HEAD` over the audited files is empty.

Evidence labels used throughout: **VERIFIED** = read from a primary Teltonika page fetched today, or decoded from real bytes, or executed against our own code. **INFERRED** = the only coherent reading of a source that does not state it outright. **UNKNOWN** = not settled by anything I could reach.

Source-access note, because three auditors hit it independently: `WebFetch` on `wiki.teltonika-gps.com` returns **403**. The routes that work are (a) `curl` with a desktop browser User-Agent against `/view/<Page>` or `index.php?title=<Page>&action=raw`, and (b) the MediaWiki API at `api.php` (`list=allpages`, `list=backlinks`, `list=embeddedin`; `list=search` is index-disabled and returns 0 hits). `r.jina.ai/<url>` also works but truncates long pages, so its *absence* answers are unreliable. `web.archive.org` is blocked in this harness. `wiki.teltonika-mobility.com` is NXDOMAIN. Almost every model page is a bare template transclusion — fetch `Template:…`, not the page.

---

## 1. Verdict in three sentences

Our Teltonika support is **strong at the frame and weak everywhere the frame stops**: framing, CRC-16/ARC, record-boundary tiling, the ACK contract and Codec 8/8E decode are correct, tested against the vendor's own worked examples and 26 real Traccar captures, and better than the reference implementation in at least two places (we echo the UDP packet id; we refuse the truncation `applySign` would otherwise silently perform) — but the moment a byte is *interpreted* rather than *located*, correctness is delegated to a third-party parser we never cross-check, a wiki column that is empty on the sensors that matter, and five independent scaling authorities that disagree by construction. The single worst hole is that **the display layer applies the dictionary's Multiplier and then its own `mV→V` rule to the same number**, so on 12 shipped tables a healthy 12.6 V backup battery renders `0.0 V` — the exact reading of dead hardware, on the parameters tab, next to a health chart on the same page that says 12.6 V. Below that sit two structural facts that generate holes faster than we close them: **an AVL id does not identify a signal** (213 of the 266 ids shared between `fmb120` and `fmb640` mean different things, and the model is not on the wire), and **every failure our own decoder invents answers ACK 0**, which is the device's record cursor — so a byte-deterministic rejection is a permanent wedge that also destroys the history queued behind it, and no metric anywhere can see one.

## 5. What our tests do not prove

The codec package sits at 537/557 statements and 253/265 branches with a 95 % threshold. Here is what that number is not evidence of.

- **Zero real-device captures.** PROJECT_PLAN §7.1 requires "captures from our 5 real devices (IMEI redacted)". There are none. Every byte the codec is tested against was written by Teltonika's documentation team, Traccar's test suite, or us.
- **We imported real packets and threw away the oracle that came with them.** The 26 Traccar captures assert only shape — frame count, codec id, record count, walker tiling, range sanity. Upstream asserts *values* (`tag1Voltage 3090`, `tag1Battery 3030`, `eyeTemp1 −11.87`, `KEY_POWER 12.873`, driver id `"12749884"`). We check none of them — including every BLE/EYE value, which is precisely the NX data nothing else checks.
- **No NX element value is asserted anywhere.** The corpus contains 24 NX elements across 11 records (AVL 385 BLE beacon list, 548, 11317 at 112 B, 256, 281, 387, four zero-length). `extractNx8e` is never unit-tested; its only proof is a round-trip against **our own encoder**, so a shared misreading in `encode.ts` + `walk.ts` passes green.
- **The 4-byte encode branch has zero coverage**, and cannot be reached by more seeds (M6). 140 four-byte values exist in the corpus, all decoded by the unverified dependency.
- **`parseMultiplier` has zero tests** — one of only two entirely uncovered functions in the package — while being live on the customer path and while the corpus really contains every shape it must refuse.
- **Priority 2 (PANIC) appears in no golden fixture.** Decoded the whole corpus: priorities present are 0 and 1. Panic is proven solely by a synthetic mutation and by the simulator — i.e. by bytes we wrote ourselves.
- **The Codec 14 nACK is never produced from wire bytes.** `parse.ts:210-212` has zero branch hits; the only nACK fixture is the ADR-011 negative one that must throw. Meanwhile `commands/reconcile.ts:63` fails a command on it.
- **Six structural defences have zero coverage**: the walker-vs-header cross-check, both codec-16 `declaredCount` rejections, the wrapped parser's record-count disagreement, its CRC rethrow, and the null-IO-value guard. These are the checks that keep a bogus cursor from being confirmed to a device.
- **No packet larger than 14 records, no `n1` near 255, no packet at 1279/1280/4095/4096 bytes.**
- **UDP is tested only against datagrams the tests build.** Zero captured UDP bytes — not the wiki's three worked examples, not Traccar's real `0049cafe0122…` datagram.
- **The simulator emits Codec 8 only, over TCP only, one record per packet (except bufferedFlood), priority 0 only, with 1/2/8-byte values and never an NX element** — while PROJECT_PLAN §7.2 mandates 8E and our FTC hardware speaks only 8E. No end-to-end path in CI ever carries a 2-byte AVL id through ingest → worker → API → web.
- **Nothing models what the ACK contract exists for** (M7): no retransmit, no duplicate, no out-of-order, no reconnect-mid-packet, no newest-first backlog, no ping, no clock drift, no device-side Codec 12/13/14 response (the worker tests feed it JSON strings).
- **The `nullIsland` scenario — written because ADR-039's defect reached the founder's screen** — is referenced by nothing except the CLI registry. No unit test, no Playwright test, no ingest test. The regression it exists to catch is reachable only by a human typing the command. `fuelTheft` has no test at all.
- **`tools/simulator` has no coverage gate** (`vitest.config.ts` is `defineConfig({})`) and `main.ts` has no test.
- **`tools/replay` does not exist** — `src/index.ts` is a two-line stub exporting its package name, and its spec asserts that name. PROJECT_PLAN §7.3's "replay real logs at 1×–100×" gate cannot run.
- **A green test pins a harvest gap as correct behaviour**: `deviceSettings.spec.ts:78-84` asserts TAT100 has no settings, which is a wiki-title bug, not a fact about TAT100.
- **The dictionary suite tests the dictionary layer, not the wire.** It is the most assertion-dense file in the package (per-table shrink floors for every table, `applySign` blast-radius assertions) and it proves nothing about decode.

## 6. Open questions

Each with what would settle it. These are not tidied into confidence.

**Protocol / device behaviour**

1. **Does a short ACK (0 < N < declared) ever advance a per-record cursor, or is resend always whole-packet?** The only normative sentence says *"module resends sent data"*. CLAUDE.md rule 4 assumes a partial accept exists. **UNKNOWN.** → One bench device plus a deliberately short ACK; read `Status → GSM Info → Records`.
2. **Is ACK 0 an ordinary mismatch (resend) or a distinct reject?** `session.ts:331` depends on the former with no citation. **UNKNOWN.** → Same bench test.
3. **What does a device do when the server ACKs *more* records than it sent?** **UNKNOWN.** → Same bench test.
4. **Does a device treat ACK 0 for a zero-record packet as satisfied or as a retry trigger?** Our own UDP test calls such a datagram a heartbeat. **UNKNOWN.** → Same.
5. **Can two genuinely distinct records carry the identical millisecond timestamp?** Four acquisition triggers can fire together. This decides whether `ON CONFLICT (device_id, fix_time, rec_hash)` silently discards a real record. **UNKNOWN.** → Decode a real high-rate capture.
6. **What timestamp does a device emit that has never had any sync, with param 107 = Always?** Zero, a fixed epoch, or the last stored RTC. **UNKNOWN.** → Bench device with no GNSS and no NTP.
7. **Do the FMB/FMC/FMM families evict oldest-first when memory is full?** Documented only for the FMA/FM36 families; our livelock reasoning cites it as if it were FMB. **INFERRED by analogy.** → An FMB-namespace statement, or a bench device filled and drained.
8. **Is the FMB6xx "Ping mode" packet also a bare `0xFF`, or framed?** **UNKNOWN.** → Capture.
9. **Does a device strictly keep one packet in flight?** Supported by a community moderator answer and by the existence of a per-packet Response Timeout; no wiki statement. **INFERRED.** → Capture two packets' timing.
10. **Are Codec 12 commands written immediately after the IMEI accept actually swallowed?** The wiki states the precondition; the errata corroborate the edge. **INFERRED.** → Simulator scenario plus a bench device.
11. **Is Traccar's 16-byte IO group real?** Gated by Traccar's own config flag, in no Teltonika document. **UNKNOWN.** → A capture where N8 is followed by something that is not a valid NX count.
12. **Does a real device reject a UDP ACK with a zeroed packet id?** We echo (correct per the wiki); Traccar zeroes. **INFERRED that echoing is right.** → Bench device on UDP with each behaviour.
13. **Does a `0xFF` ping arrive over UDP?** flespi shipped a fix for it. **UNKNOWN for us.** → Capture.
14. **Does `lvcansimpletacho 0|1` shift payload bytes?** The wiki says only "add or remove simpletacho start byte". If it shifts, values for ids 133–150 depend on a config flag nothing announces. **UNKNOWN.** → Vendor answer or a bench adapter.

**Wiki self-contradictions (CLAUDE.md rule 8 says the wiki wins — it cannot win against itself)**

15. **Does Codec 8E's `N of Total` include NX?** The Codec page's formula says no; 11 of 11 NX-bearing corpus records and Teltonika's own EYE Beacon example say yes. **VERIFIED contradiction, no ADR** (C1). → The ADR; the decision is already evidenced.
16. **Codec 16's total-IO-count width**: the differences table says 2 bytes, the structure table and the decoded example say 1. **Settled by decode (1 byte)**, unrecorded.
17. **Max AVL packet size for FMB630/640/FM63XY**: 255 B on one page, 512 B on another, and a real 645-byte record exists. **UNKNOWN**; do not use either as a buffer bound.
18. **AVL 56 multiplier**: ×10 vs ×0.1 across Teltonika's own pages. **INFERRED ×10** from SAE J1979. → Vendor answer or a decoded FMx150 sample.
19. **AVL 70 on FT/AT**: multiplier cell empty, range implies ×0.1. **INFERRED.** → A decoded FT sample.
20. **ATC/ATM BLE ids 13227–13232 and 13335–13339**: the ATX table gives literal dashes for width, type, range and multiplier. **The wiki does not answer this at all.** → A decoded ATC704 sample, or the Configurator TCT bundle.

## 7. The order I would fix these in

Reasoning is *who is hurt and how loudly*, not severity rank. A silent wrong number on a screen today outranks a wedge that no customer owns yet; a wedge that loses history outranks a feature nobody has bought.

**0 — Fix the fixes (before anything else, half a day).** I4a and M1a. Two of this audit's own recommendations are unsafe as written: the ACK-0 park would turn a Redis outage into fleet-wide deletion under a positive acknowledgement, and the `default:` catch-all would ACK a record count to command frames — the exact desync M1 exists to stop. *Who is hurt:* everyone, by us, in the act of fixing something else. Nothing below ships until these two guards are in.

**1 — S1, the double-scaled voltage (half a day).** One place in one file, and it is producing a wrong number on a customer's screen **right now** on 12 tables. `0.0 V` is not an odd reading — it is the reading of dead hardware, and the same page contradicts itself two rows apart. *Who is hurt:* FMx6xx truck fleets and every FMx150/FMB010 customer with an analogue sensor, plus our own support engineers, who cannot tell which of the two numbers on the page to believe. It is also the cheapest possible demonstration that the audit found real things.

**2 — I3, the `0xFF` ping (half a day).** Reproduced, two lines in the framer plus a frame kind. *Who is hurt:* every fleet whose carrier closes idle links — i.e. the normal M2M case — with a reconnect storm at the ping rate and parked vehicles going dark. It also fixes the second half of the same bug (good frames discarded with the garbage behind them), which nothing else will.

**3 — I5 + I14 + I15 + I16, the "we cannot name the problem" batch (one day, together).** TLS diagnosis, a refused-connection counter, the codec on the row, and the four transport settings surfaced read-only. None of these fixes a bug; all four convert a week of support guesswork into one sentence. *Who is hurt:* every customer whose device does not appear, and every support engineer who currently has four indistinguishable causes and no instrument. Do them as one batch because they share the same shape and the same reviewer.

**4 — I1 + I2 + I4, the wedge and the loss (two to three days, gated on step 0).** Codec 15/17 as message codecs, Codec 16 decoded, and the k-consecutive park with its guard. *Who is hurt:* FMB630/FM6300 fleets losing every record under a positive ACK, and FMx6xx professional installs with a serial device wedged permanently. Ordered here rather than first because these are *silences*, and a silent customer is one who has not bought yet — but they are the holes that lose data we can never get back, so they must not slip further.

**5 — S2 + S10, the fallback table and the decoding-table column (two to three days).** Decode with an empty dictionary on any fallback, and persist the table that decoded the row. *Who is hurt:* any FMx6xx truck during a Redis blip or before rehydrate — 4.5 litres of fuel from an accelerator pedal, a 7 500 kg axle load firing `fuel_theft`. This ranks fifth rather than second because the fix is structural and the same column unlocks S10, S15 and every future per-table correction; doing it before the cheap wins would stall the whole audit.

**6 — The generator batch: S3, S6, S7, S8, S14, S16, C3, C4 (two days, all in `tools/avl-dict` + `dictionaries.ts`).** Offset field, inverted-multiplier detection, scale-in-unit normalisation, sentinel extraction, range-derived multipliers, folded backend corrections, one corpus capture, sanitised group cells. *Who is hurt:* every temperature reader on the platform — the truck operator seeing 130 °C coolant, the reefer operator seeing `20 10*°C`, the cold-chain operator seeing a steady 85 °C from a probe that fell off. Batched because they are one file, one review, one regeneration, and because each one alone is too small to justify the CI churn.

**7 — I7 + I8 + I9, the semantic index (three to four days).** One mechanism closes the ATC blindness, the blank CAN panel and the empty fuel graph, and lets the API stop accepting rules the hardware cannot satisfy. *Who is hurt:* ATC/ATM trailer fleets whose battery alert can never fire, and the FMC650 + ALL-CAN300 customer who bought the hardware for a screen that renders empty. This is the largest single change in the list and the one that stops the *class* rather than the instances — every future "id 87 means something else here" defect is answered by it.

**8 — I10 + I11 + I12, the catalogue gaps (one day).** Two suffixes in the params generator, four `model` fields in the seed, a re-run of the dictionary generator, and a CI check comparing our catalogues against the wiki's page list. *Who is hurt:* FMB641/FMC640/FMM640 truck buyers told in writing that their tracker has no CAN support, our earliest customers on legacy profiles, and anyone who buys an ATM774. Cheap, embarrassing, and visible in a demo.

**9 — S11 + S12, the time and ordering pair (three to four days).** Geofence and rule replay on the `late` drain; the backward-drift signal. *Who is hurt:* any customer with a geofence whose vehicle passes through a tunnel — their dwell reports are short by the outage and the trip history looks perfect, so nobody will ever report it — and any customer with a failing RTC, whose map shows a truck parked where it isn't while the fleet page says online. Ninth because both are genuinely hard and neither has a cheap interim, not because they matter less.

**10 — M7 + M6, the harness (two days).** Four simulator scenarios (`retransmit`, `backlogNewest`, `networkPing`, `driftedClock`) and an explicit width channel in the encoder. *Who is hurt:* us, next time. These are what would have caught steps 2 and 9 before a customer did, and M6 is a hard prerequisite for regression-testing anything from step 6 that keys on wire width. Placed after the fixes rather than before only because the fixes are already known — from here on, the harness is what finds the next one.

**11 — The remainder: M1–M5, M8–M10, S4, S5, S13, S15, S17, C1, C2, C5, C6.** Ordered inside this block by whoever is holding the ticket: M1/M2/M5 if a customer is using commands or cameras; S4 if a UDP fleet lands; M10 if driver identification is sold; C1 and C2 whenever someone is already in those files. *Who is hurt:* narrower populations, or nobody yet. The two worth pulling forward on a customer trigger are **M10** (driver identification is a feature people buy specifically, and it is dead) and **S4** (the first serious UDP fleet turns a bit flip into a phantom trip nobody will report).
